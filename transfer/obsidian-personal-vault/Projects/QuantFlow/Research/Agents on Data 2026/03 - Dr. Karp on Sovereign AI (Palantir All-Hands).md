**This is not a technical talk.** No slides, no architecture — an internal all-hands about positioning. Filed anyway, because three things in it bear directly on QuantFlow, and one of them is the sharpest argument for the whole project I've read.

## 1 · Why the ontology exists at all — the origin story

The most valuable 30 seconds, and it's historical:

> *"Ontology came from Gotham because we couldn't actually get the NLP stuff to work. You had to extend it beyond what we could do… we had to have some way of **enriching the knowledge of the underlying system**. Ontology came to be."*

**The ontology was not a design ideal. It was what you build when the language model alone doesn't work.** That is the same conclusion Coyle reaches from first principles ([[Why Agentic Systems Need Ontologies (Frank Coyle, AIE 2026)]]) and Eifrem reaches from enterprise practice ([[04 - Thinner Agents on a Smarter Substrate (Emil Eifrem, Neo4j)]]) — but Karp is reporting it as **something that already happened, years ago, under pressure.** Three independent arrivals at the same place is about as much confirmation as an architectural bet ever gets.

He notes the same lineage produced the Forward Deployed Engineer role: the ontology and the FDE are two halves of one answer to "the model can't do this alone."

![[aod03-karp-t0425.jpg]]

## 2 · The alpha-migration argument — the QuantFlow-relevant core

On the frontier labs, and unusually direct:

> *"They are unwilling to respond to any questions about how do you build the models **without ripping off someone's IP and migrating their alpha to your model and then competing against them.** And they do this over and over. So the clients really feel this."*

And the customer's version of the complaint:

> *"Why am I paying for tokens that lead to this weird kind of slop that only makes the least productive people happy? **And simultaneously migrate the value of my business to a third party.**"*

> **Read this against QuantFlow directly.** QuantFlow's entire purpose is generating betting alpha. Alpha is the most migration-sensitive asset there is — it is worth something *only while few people have it*. A research console that ships hypotheses, backtests and evaluations through someone else's API is handing over the one thing it exists to produce.
>
> The Kernel is local SQLite. Artifacts are local bytes. The event log never leaves the machine. **That was chosen for truth-ownership reasons, and it turns out to be the correct commercial posture too** — for exactly the reason Karp's enterprise customers are angry about. This is the strongest available argument against "just use a hosted agent platform," and it should probably be written into `START_HERE.md` as a stated reason for local-first, which currently reads as a technical preference rather than a strategic one.

## 3 · "You own the means of production"

The line he tells the company to answer every question with:

> *"We are building a stack where **you will maintain the means of your own production. You own the means of production.** We participate because you succeed."*

Paired with the business-model claim: Palantir is *"paid as a derivative of value creation for the enterprises we serve. We do not have any other way of getting paid. Every other company wants to outsource the risk of failure to the client because they know it's going to fail — because to succeed you'd need a full stack from compute all the way up to model training, and you'd have to absorb the risk of that not working."*

**For a solo founder this is oddly clarifying.** You are not buying sovereignty from a vendor; you are simply *already sovereign*, because you own the whole stack by default. The thing Palantir sells to a Fortune 20 bank at enormous cost — a governed ontology on infrastructure you control — is the thing QuantFlow has for free by being one person with a laptop and a SQLite file.

## 4 · The intellectual move — Aufhebung

The framing device he keeps returning to: Palantir's products are built by taking two things assumed to be opposites and putting them in a frame where the contradiction dissolves (the German *Aufhebung*). Gotham's version was **data protection + anti-terror**, reframed as both serving "the value of life — being alive and having rights."

> *"In 20 years of experience, when the doors close, none of the caricatures are true."*

QuantFlow has a structurally identical move available and hasn't named it: **agent autonomy vs. auditability.** The assumed trade-off is that agents are useful in proportion to how unconstrained they are. The Kernel's answer is that the constraint *is* what makes them useful — a governed world model is what lets an agent act without a human checking every step. Worth writing down as doctrine; it's the same shape as Coyle's *"the agent proposes, the ontology permits."*

## 5 · Where he's talking about a different game

![[aod03-karp-t1255.jpg]]

> *"There's no time to start a new business. By the time you start a new business, we have educated the market about their own interests."*

True for competing with Palantir on sovereign enterprise deployments. **Not applicable to QuantFlow**, which is not selling a governed ontology to governments — it's one operator's research console for one market. Noted so this doesn't get mis-filed as discouragement: it's a claim about a market QuantFlow isn't in.
